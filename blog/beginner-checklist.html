The 10 Essentials (Non‑Negotiable)

These items can save your life in an emergency. Always carry them, even on short day hikes.

1. Navigation

Map, compass, GPS (or phone with offline maps). Don't rely solely on your phone – batteries die.

2. Headlamp

Even if you plan to finish before dark, things happen. Carry a lightweight headlamp with extra batteries.

3. Sun Protection

Hat, sunglasses, sunscreen (SPF 30+). Sunburn can ruin a trip and increase risk of heat illness.

4. First Aid

Basic kit with bandages, antiseptic, blister care (moleskin!), pain relievers, and any personal meds.

5. Knife / Multi-tool

For gear repairs, food prep, or emergencies. A small Swiss Army knife or multi-tool is perfect.

Waterproof matches, lighter, or fire starter. Crucial for warmth and signaling if stranded.

Clothing & Footwear

Dressing properly is half the battle. Follow the layering system: base layer (moisture-wicking), mid layer (insulation), outer layer (wind/rain protection).

Footwear

For beginners, a pair of comfortable, broken-in trail runners or lightweight hiking boots is best. Avoid heavy boots unless you're carrying a heavy pack.

Beginner Tips

  • Start small: Choose easy, well-marked trails under 5 miles.
  • Check the weather: Always look at the forecast before heading out.
  • Tell someone your plan: Share your route and expected return time with a friend.
  • Pace yourself: Hike at a pace where you can hold a conversation.
  • Leave no trace: Pack out all trash, stay on trail.

FAQ

Do I need expensive gear to start?

Absolutely not. Start with affordable, quality basics and upgrade as you gain experience. Our budget gear guide can help.

How do I break in new boots?

Wear them around the house, then on short walks, before hitting the trail. Never take new boots on a long hike.

What should I eat on the trail?

High-energy, lightweight foods like trail mix, energy bars, jerky, and dried fruit. Avoid heavy, greasy meals.
